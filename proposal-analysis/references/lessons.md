# Failure modes that overturn headline numbers

Each item below can reverse or halve a proposal's central claim after it has been written. Check them before quoting.

1. **Incomplete evaluation.** A fit to one reaction channel can push parameters (for example partial widths of an open channel) to values that data in another channel exclude. Fit all channels that constrain the same compound system before computing designs or decision fractions.
2. **Unused normalization-free data.** Angular distributions, polarizations and shapes may already decide what the proposal claims its measurement will decide. Search for them and include them.
3. **Normalization convention.** Before interpreting a fitted scale factor, confirm whether it multiplies data or model; the direction of a "tension" flips otherwise.
4. **Data-file columns.** A mislabelled or malformed column (for example a laboratory energy read as centre-of-mass) can silently drop or misplace points in a fit. Count the points each code actually uses and compare with the file.
5. **Screening and frames.** Compare bare with bare and screened with screened; state laboratory or centre-of-mass energy everywhere.
6. **Importance-weight degeneracy.** See sampling-checks.md.
7. **Best fits instead of posteriors.** Separations and decision fractions quoted from best fits ignore the predictive spread.
8. **Dilution and double counting of shared systematics.** See covariances.md.
9. **Within- versus cross-system correlation.** Claim novelty only for what a joint multichannel fit does not already do.
10. **Hand-picked comparisons.** A back-test without a stated rule invites the reply "selection". See backtests.md.
11. **Optimistic power.** Independent-error simulations overstate power; see design-and-decisions.md.
12. **Unverified sources.** A value "quoted by" an evaluation may be a single measurement; read the primary source, record page and line.
13. **Small effects stated as large.** Give the observable's uncertainty before and after; a modest effect stated honestly is more credible than an inflated one.
14. **Convergence.** A growing autocorrelation time means the tails are not known; say so.
