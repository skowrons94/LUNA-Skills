# Scientific feasibility presentations

## Narrative

Build the deck around one decision. A strong sequence is:

1. Minimal title slide with reaction, channel, energy, decision, presenter, and affiliation.
2. The two to four unknowns that control feasibility.
3. Input cross section or rate and its defensible working assumption.
4. Validation against measured yields or calibration.
5. The dominant background or detector limitation.
6. Signal and background spectra in the relevant angular or energy windows.
7. Option comparison with count rate, efficiency, signal-to-background ratio, and time to precision.
8. Preferred geometry or operating region.
9. A conclusion that answers the opening question and names the next test.

Put detailed derivations, alternative assumptions, and diagnostic plots in backup slides. Do not end with an empty “Thank you” slide when a conclusion or open-question slide can carry the discussion.

## Copy

- Give each slide one claim and express it in a takeaway title when the evidence supports one.
- Use short, direct scientific language. Keep equations only when they change the audience's interpretation.
- Put assumptions beside the number they affect.
- Use the same names, units, colors, and energy-frame notation as the report.
- Never paste a report paragraph onto a slide. Replace it with a claim, a figure or table, and one implication.

## Visual system

Use the `research-slides` skill for the visual system, components, Manim animations, rendering and the deck audit. Its house-style reference covers the default conference layout and a compact working-group variant for feasibility decks (10 × 5.62 in, white, Cambria titles in orange-red `#C25E2E`, Calibri body, flat tables, thin warm-grey footer rule).

Keep colour semantic and stable across plots within a study. For example, in a charged-particle feasibility study: blues for alpha channels, reds for proton channels, greens for direct deuterium background, and magenta/wine for the knock-on chain.

## Figures

Use report and presentation figures derived from the same results, but render each for its medium. Presentation labels, legends, and annotations must remain legible at room scale. Prefer one main figure or a disciplined comparison; avoid a wall of small diagnostic axes.

For spectra, keep the legend outside the plotting area when possible. Clearly mark the signal window, background definition, energy or angular selection, and the quantitative takeaway. If the conclusion depends on angle, do not show only an angle-summed spectrum.

## Verification

Inspect every source slide when following an existing PPTX. Preserve its master, layouts, fonts, footer, and structural placeholders. Render every final slide and inspect it at full size for overlap, clipping, unreadable labels, inconsistent units, and unsupported claims. Put sources for non-trivial claims and external assets in the speaker notes.
