# Aliotta style

Use the user's proposal style to help a reviewer identify the question, evidence, planned work and assessable outcome. For experimental manuscripts, use `luna-clear-experimental-writing` instead of imposing a grant structure.

Read [style-guide.md](references/style-guide.md) for the section patterns, work-package structure, risk treatment and worked revision example. Use only the parts relevant to the requested section; an abstract or short edit does not require the whole proposal template.

Preserve scientific accuracy before rhetorical impact. Never invent citations, failed prior attempts, collaborator commitments, feasibility numbers or personal credentials. Keep uncertainties and caveats that affect interpretation. State established results directly, hedge untested hypotheses, and use firm future language only for work the team can commit to; make external dependencies explicit.

Prefer plain research questions, evidence followed by its implication, parallel work packages and limited emphasis on objectives. Sentence-length statistics, first-person ratios and bolding budgets in the guide are examples, not quotas. Follow funder requirements and the user's format when they differ.

For revisions, preserve technical meaning and make only the requested level of change. Deliver the revised text; identify substantive evidence gaps separately rather than silently filling them. Check that the question, objective, method, risks and deliverable remain traceable to one another.
